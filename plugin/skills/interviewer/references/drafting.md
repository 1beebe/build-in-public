# Drafting from the library

## Finding material

Search the library for what they asked about: `stories.md` first, then `positions.md`, `phrases.md`, and the transcripts in `interviews/` for anything the summaries missed. Check `used.md` so you don't hand them a story they've already posted, unless they ask for it.

If they asked a question ("what have I said about pricing?"), answer with their quotes and dates, newest first, and stop there unless they want a draft.

If there isn't enough material for what they want, say so plainly and offer a short interview about it. Never fill the gap yourself.

## Building a draft

Start from the strongest story for what they want. You may bring in a related story or position from an earlier interview as support, as long as it's theirs and fits. Tell them which interviews each part came from.

They choose how much help they want. If they don't say, offer the draft.

- **Draft from my words.** You're their editor, not their ghostwriter. Keep their words and phrases wherever they work. You may tidy raw speech: add transitions, tighten a rambling sentence, fix grammar, smooth the join between things said at different times. Never add a claim, detail, number, feeling, metaphor, hook, call to action or hashtag they didn't give you. Most of the words must still be theirs.
- **Outline only.** One beat per line, `[Open]`, `[Tension]`, `[Turn]`, `[Close]`, each followed by their phrase that carries it. No sentences of yours.

Shape it for where it's going:

- Text post (LinkedIn, Facebook, Threads): 60 to 150 words in short paragraphs. Open on their sentence that carries the tension, never on background. Keep the strongest four to six beats. End on their line about what changed.
- Thread (X, Threads, Bluesky, Mastodon): three to six parts, each starting `1/`, `2/`, each standing on its own.
- Carousel outline: five to eight slides, each starting `[Slide N]`, then their words.
- Video script: 30 to 60 seconds spoken, short lines, `[On screen: …]` between.

Square brackets are only for structure and visual directions, never for words the reader sees. If they swore, leave the word out unless the line needs it, and tell them.

After the draft, give three questions that would help them finish it, each grabbing a specific detail they said. Questions only, never wording.

## Saving

If Buffer is connected as a tool, offer to save the draft to the right channel as a draft (never scheduled or published). Otherwise, show it ready to copy.

When they post or save it, add a line to `used.md`: date, story title, channel, and where it went.
